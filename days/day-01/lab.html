Maturity lab

Part A runs the pack and a virtual counterpart in the browser, with the maturity level and the sensor of your choice. Part B classifies six systems as models, shadows or twins. Part C assigns responsibilities to the parts of the reference architecture. Parts D and E repeat two calculations of the lecture step by step, the energy balance of the pack and the root mean square error (RMSE) of the shadow, with sliders for their numbers. In these parts every intermediate value is rounded, and the next step continues with the rounded value, as in a calculation by hand.

Part A: The maturity bench

Configure the virtual counterpart of the pack and the sensor that feeds it, then press Run and add to scoreboard. The plot shows the true temperature of the pack and what the counterpart believes. The twin may switch the fan; the model and the shadow only watch. Try a sensor bias of minus five degrees with the twin and compare the last two readouts.

Part D: The energy balance of the pack, step by step

The worked examples of the lecture: the heating of the pack, its cooling by the air and the fan, the rate of change of its temperature, one Euler step, the steady state and the time constant. The sliders set the current, the cell temperature, the fan duty, the temperature of the air and the time step. The parameters are those of the pack: \(R = 0.045\) ohm, \(m c_p = 9000\) J/K, \(hA_0 = 2.2\) W/K and \(hA_1 = 12\) W/K. The left panel compares the two powers, and the right panel follows the temperature from now on, with the current and the fan held.

Continue in Colab, section 1: the full model of the pack over a trip of two hours.

Part E: The error of the shadow and the synchronisation interval

The formula of the lecture for the error of a shadow that overwrites its belief with every reading, term by term: the noise \(\sigma\) and the bias \(b\) of the sensor, the drift rate \(v\) of the model and the synchronisation interval \(\Delta t_{\mathrm{sync}}\). The drift rate is set in degrees per minute. The figure draws the whole curve on a logarithmic axis, as the animation of the lecture does, and marks the chosen interval. Beyond about twenty minutes the formula no longer holds, and the curve is dashed there.
