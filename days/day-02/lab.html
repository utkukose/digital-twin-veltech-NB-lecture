Simulation lab

Part A integrates the thermal model of the pack with three methods and collects every run in a diagram of error against cost. Part B computes a stability limit and an order by hand. Part C chooses the kind of simulation for six systems. Part D repeats one step of the three methods of the lecture, slope by slope, and Part E runs the event list of a small charging depot, event by event, both with sliders for their numbers. In these parts every intermediate value is rounded, and the next step continues with the rounded value, as in a calculation by hand.

Part A: The integrator workbench

Choose an integration method, a step size and a fan duty, from 0 for off to 1 for full power, and integrate the thermal model of the pack over two hours. The reference is the classical Runge-Kutta method of order 4, abbreviated RK4, with a step of one second. Every run is added to the scoreboard, where the plot of error against the number of derivative evaluations, the work-precision diagram of the lecture, shows which method buys accuracy most cheaply.

Part B: Accuracy and stability by hand

The pack has a heat capacity of 9000 J/K, a passive cooling conductance of 2.2 W/K and an extra 12 W/K at full fan. The stability limit of explicit Euler is twice the heat capacity divided by the total conductance.

Continue in Colab, section 3: watch Euler fail beyond its stability limit.

Part D: One step with Euler, Heun and Runge-Kutta

The worked example of the lecture: one step of the thermal model of the pack with each of the three methods, compared with the exact solution. The slope at a temperature \(T\) is \(f(T)\)​\({} = (I^2 R - (hA_0 + hA_1 u)(T - 35))\)​\({} / 9000\) degrees per second, with \(R = 0.045\) ohm, \(hA_0 = 2.2\) W/K, \(hA_1 = 12\) W/K and air at 35 °C. The sliders set the step, the start temperature, the current and the fan duty. The figure draws the exact curve and the straight line along which each method moves through the step.

Continue in Colab, section 2: the three methods over one hour and their measured orders.

Part E: The event list of a small depot, event by event

A discrete-event simulation of the charging depot of the lecture, small enough to follow by hand: six vehicles arrive with the states of charge 0.20, 0.35, 0.10, 0.40, 0.25 and 0.30 and are charged to 0.90 with a current \(I_c\), so that a charge takes \(t_c\)​\({} = (0.9 - \mathrm{SoC}_{\mathrm{in}}) \times 60 / I_c\) hours. Each step takes the earliest event from the list, moves the clock to its time and carries it out. At equal times an end of charge goes first. The sliders set the number of bays, the charging current and the spacing of the arrivals, a factor on the arrival times 0, 0.3, 0.5, 1.1, 1.3 and 1.6 hours. The chart shows the bays and the line up to the clock of the current step.

Continue in Colab, section 5: the depot with 60 vehicles a day and its event list in code.
