Intelligence lab

Part A runs a control room in which four controllers set the fan of the pack and you set the weights of the objective that ranks them. Part B matches the needs of an operator with the services of the twin. Part C computes a cumulative sum by hand. Parts D, E and F repeat three services of the lecture step by step, with sliders for their numbers: a prediction of a Gaussian process, a remaining useful life from a fitted line and one decision of the twin in the loop. In these parts every intermediate value is rounded, and the next step continues with the rounded value, as in a calculation by hand.

Part A: The control room

Four controllers drive the fan of the pack over a two-hour trip with a hot afternoon load. Set the weights of the objective, which put a degree-hour above 44 °C, a kilowatt-hour (kWh) of fan energy and a milliampere-hour (mAh) of lost capacity on one scale, then run all four. A degree-hour is one degree above 44 °C for one hour. Without cooling the fan stays off, and the always-on fan runs at full duty. The fixed rule switches the fan fully on above 42 °C and off again below 40 °C. The twin in the loop plans once per minute: It looks ten minutes ahead with three fan settings, 0, 0.5 and 1, and applies the cheapest one. The weights are a business decision: Watch how they change the ranking.

Part C: A cumulative sum by hand

The standardised residuals of five periods are 0.1, 0.4, 0.6, 0.5 and 0.7. The cumulative sum starts at zero and adds each residual minus an allowance of 0.25, but never falls below zero. The alarm is raised when the sum exceeds 1.0.

Continue in Colab, section 5: the cumulative sum against a fixed limit on the pack.

Part D: A Gaussian-process prediction, step by step

The worked example of the lecture: a Gaussian process with one input, the current, the squared exponential kernel and no noise. The mean of the training outputs is 50 °C, and the first training point is 53 °C at 40 A. A second training point, 60 °C at 55 A, can be added. The sliders set the query, the length scale \(\ell\) and the amplitude \(\sigma_f\). The figure draws the prediction over all currents with a band of two standard deviations on each side.

Continue in Colab, section 2: a Gaussian process with three inputs trained on 120 simulations.

Part E: A remaining useful life from a fitted line

The estimate of the lecture on a short history: six measurements of the capacity, one every 20 trips from trip 0 to trip 100. A straight line is fitted by least squares and followed down to the end-of-life level of 48 Ah. The true pack loses a fixed amount of capacity per trip, and from a knee on it loses twice as much. The sliders set this loss, the measurement noise and the trip of the knee. The figure compares the extrapolated line with the true capacity.

Continue in Colab, section 6: the same estimate on 180 trips, with a bootstrap interval.

Part F: One decision of the twin in the loop

The planner of the lecture, shortened so that every number can be followed by hand: three candidate fan duties instead of six, three explicit Euler steps of 100 s instead of fifteen steps of 20 s, a constant current of 40 A and air at 35 °C, with the calibrated parameters of Day 3. The cost of a candidate adds the squared excess above 44 °C and the energy of the fan, each times the length of a step in minutes. The ageing term of the lecture is left out here. The sliders set the temperature at the moment of the decision and the weight of the fan energy.
